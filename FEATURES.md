# Features

## Available now

* **Devices:** a list kept between runs; work area, travel in Z, safe height, speed of moves, spindle range and wait, machine zero, units (mm or inch), notes; up to eight cameras (address or camera of the computer, rotation) with a live Camera tab; add, edit, duplicate, delete, export and import (checked strictly); the stand-in Dummy device.
* **Tabs:** every open drawing has a tab above the work area (File → New and Open make one, up to 10; a project that is open is shown, a blank tab is used as it is). A dot marks a tab with unsaved work, the cross closes it (asking Save, Discard or Cancel when there is unsaved work), a middle click closes it too. Every tab with unsaved work has its own recovery file, and after a crash the start screen offers to recover them all, each in a tab. Closing the program asks about each tab with unsaved work in turn: the tab is shown, you answer, it is closed, the next one follows; Cancel stops the closing. Ctrl+1 … Ctrl+9 and Ctrl+0 show the tabs, Ctrl+W closes the shown one (the last tab closes the program).
* **Device and Control tabs:** under the connection and the position (a line between), Home, Unlock, Pause and Resume are a block of 2 × 2 with icons (Home blue, Unlock red, Pause orange, Resume green); the same block is at the top of the Control tab, above the position, and under them a wide STOP button that is live only while the machine works. The Zero buttons are a grid of 2 × 2, and the sections run: position, zero, Move, the jog buttons, Speed, Probe, Spindle (its Switch on and Switch off in a row of two under the speed).
* **Layers:** 64 layers with name, colour, operation, bit, depth, pass depth, step over, feeds and spindle speed; switch each on and off, show and hide; put the selected shapes on a layer.
* **Operations:** cut outside the line, cut inside the line, follow the line (open and closed), pocket, drill with pecks.
* **Bit library:** ready-made bits and your own, kept for every device; kinds: flat end mill, ball nose, bull nose, V-bit, engraving bit, tapered ball nose, drill; a picture of every kind with the letter of each dimension where it is measured; feeds and speeds as starting points; import and export of bits as JSON; a project carries the bits it uses.
* **Material:** wood (light, medium, dark), metal, plastic (any colour), thickness; an optional **first pass** (0.1 mm by default) along the edges of every layer before its normal passes, to break the top of the material cleanly.
* **Toolpaths:** offsets by the radius of the bit, passes down to the depth, rings for pockets, a safe height, estimate of the length and the time, and warnings.
* **The machine:** a GRBL controller on a serial port or a network socket (port, baud rate, the GRBL version from 0.7 to 1.1 and newer, streaming, homing or clearing an alarm after connecting, reconnecting by itself); Connect, Home, Unlock, Pause / Resume, Frame (the box of the work at the safe height), Start and Stop, a **Console** tab (the traffic with the controller, a line to send a command) and the position; Save G-code on the control bar.
* **Rotary attachment:** chuck or rollers, workpiece diameter, microsteps per turn; the drawing may not be higher than the way round the workpiece (warning).
* **Toolpaths button** (in the bar over the work area, with Preview… and Camera view): the paths drawn over the work area; **Camera view** window of the live camera. START and STOP, and under them **Frame** (always the left one) and **From line…** with icons, in a row of two, are in the Control tab.
* **3D view:** the material as it is milled (the cuts carved into its top, with the real shape of each bit, up to the slider; **Export** of that piece as an STL model), the paths of the bit over it, moves at the safe height, turn, zoom, move, reset, from above as a plan, an animation (play, pause, speed ×0.25 to ×25, repeat) with a progress slider and the bit marked; the shown area is 10 % larger than the project.
* **Job options** (in the Control tab, under Frame and From line…; this session only, together or alone): **One element at a time** cuts each element (a group, even over several layers, or a shape that is in no group) completely, with all its layers and passes, before the next one, the nearest to the machine's zero first (meant for layers with the same bit); **Selection only** cuts just the shapes that are selected when the G-code is saved or the job is started. Both change the G-code, the preview, the estimate and the frame.
* **G-code** for GRBL: spindle and wait, rapid and cutting moves with feeds, the zero of the machine, a stop to change the bit (`M0`).
* **Project file** `.cre` (shared with LightCreator): the drawing, the description and the `mill` section; the sections of other programs are kept as they were.
* The window of LightCreator's look: own frame and title bar, start screen, control bar, status bar, tabs (Properties, Layers, Material, Device, Clipart, Description, Camera), the clipart gallery (310 included pictures, your own, online search), the colour schemes, fonts and the 30 languages of baseCreator for the shared texts.
* Reading SVG, PDF and Illustrator (PDF-compatible) files and LightBurn projects (`.lbrn2`: shapes, text outlines, bitmaps) and UGS Designer designs (`.ugsd`: shapes, text, pictures; the cut types, depths, feeds and spindle speeds become layers) into a scene of shapes.
* **Pictures:** File → Import → Import image… (or drop a PNG, JPG, BMP, GIF or WebP file on the window) opens the import dialog (rotate, flip, brightness, contrast, gamma, auto levels, negative, size); Arrange → **Adjust image…** changes a picture on the page, and **Trace image…** turns its dark (or light) areas into outlines (threshold, speckle filter, simplification, curve smoothing) that are cut or drawn like any other shape. The picture itself is only shown. The import and adjust dialog follows the size of the window and has a **Levels** column: a histogram of the greys with two sliders for the range (black and white point). The trace dialog zooms (scroll wheel) and moves (drag) its preview. The tool palette shrinks its buttons when the window is too low for it.
* **Looking for a newer release** on GitHub when the program starts (switch in Settings → Program options…) and by hand in Help → Check for updates…; it only asks which release is newest and opens the download page, nothing is installed by itself.
* **Layer bar** under the editor, the same in all programs: one row of the 64 layer colours (arrows or the wheel scroll it; a dot marks layers that have shapes). Click a colour to put the selected shapes on that layer (or, with nothing selected, to draw on it); double-click for its options.
* **Own G-code and work coordinates:** the device settings have a page *G-code* (own lines at the start and the end of the program and at every change of the bit and of the operation) and choose a work coordinate system **G54 … G59** with the zero of the program (X, Y), also used by the frame.
* **Plugins** (Settings → Plugins…, shared with the other programs on baseCreator): clipart, machine presets, translations, importers of other file formats, shape generators with a form, and G-code postprocessors, as folders or zips; scripts run in a sandbox (Rhai) and the postprocessor output is checked. An **official list** of plugins (all programs' plugins, Install / Update with a checksum, updates checked only when you ask or switch it on) is in Plugins… → Official plugins. Every plugin that has something to set (a postprocessor) has a **tab of its own** in the right-hand panel; the official plugins are translated into Polish, German, Italian, Finnish, Chinese and Hindi.
* The editor of baseCreator: drawing tools, selection, node editing, undo and redo, copy and paste, group, align, flip, rotate, order, grid and snapping.
* **Machine control:** the **Control** tab (work and machine position, zero of X, Y, Z and G54 … G59, jogging by steps or by holding a button, keyboard jogging, go to X0 Y0 at the safe height, probing Z and the X / Y edges with a touch plate or a 3D probe with the tool diameter taken into account, macros with variables and G-code before and after every job); the job stops at a change of the bit (a window, a Z probe, continue / stop); **Start from line…**; the program can be checked in the controller's check mode first; the settings of the controller (`$$`) with search, a filter for what differs from typical, writes after a question, an automatic backup before the first change, backup to and restore from a file, factory reset behind a typed word; calibration of steps per millimetre and of the squareness of the axes; diagnostics (input pins, firmware, jobs, running time, alarms, errors, a zip with report, settings, console and program) and maintenance reminders; alarms and errors of GRBL explained in 30 languages; a device can be read from the controller (travel, spindle, speed) and started from a usual machine size; **copies of the settings of the controller** are kept with the device (made at the first connection and whenever the settings differ from the newest copy, with a message listing what changed; by hand, restore and delete from the firmware window; part of the backup of the program); a **tool length sensor** (menu Machine): its place in machine coordinates, the reference tool measured on it, and every new bit measured at a pause of the job so that it gets the Z of the reference tool. It has been tested on a TwoTrees TTC450Pro, not on other machines.
* **Backup:** Help → Download backup… saves a zip with the settings of MillCreator and the shared clipart gallery; Help → Restore from backup… reads it back (settings replaced after a confirmation, pictures added to the gallery, a backup of another program refused). The same entries as in LightCreator.
* **Safe profiles (Stage 1 of the MillMage list):** a layer chooses the **direction** of a profile or a pocket (as drawn, climb or conventional; a hole of a part goes the other way round the part), **stock to leave** on the walls with a **finishing pass** at the full depth, and how the bit goes **down**: plunge, a **zigzag ramp**, a **ramp along the contour** or a **helix** (in a pocket, where it fits; with its own ramp feed, which a bit of the library can give). **Tabs** (bridges) hold a part while it is cut out: full (the bit rises above the material), partial (to the height of the tab) or ramp (with a slope), spread evenly by a spacing with a fewest and a most, none on holes, or placed by hand on the work area (click to add, drag to move, click to remove); only the passes below the top of the tab are raised. Warnings: a ramp steeper than 45°, tabs as high as the cut, a helix that does not fit.
* **Pictures are engraved:** a picture put on a layer switches the layer to the Picture operation: dithered rows, dots, or rows whose depth follows the tone, with a choice of the dithering and the angle of the rows; **3D relief** (as in molino): the grey level is the depth, cut in passes with the shape of the bit taken into account, and **Export relief as STL…** saves the carved piece as a closed solid.
* **Right-click menu:** on a shape (copy, paste, duplicate, delete, group, lock, flip, turn, arrange, shape operations: union, intersection, subtract, exclusive or, offset, round corners, convert to curves; adjust and trace a picture), the same in all programs.
